### Definitions
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What is a Re-entry point in evacuation zones?
ANSWER: A Re-entry point is an area within a previous evacuation zone where residents may return according to temporary entrance times which are assigned by officials to property owners who are required to abide by stipulations and requirements established by authorities.

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: What is a Safe Refuge Area (CRA) and what are some examples?
ANSWER: A Safe Refuge Area is a temporary, safe location to shelter citizens until either a safe evacuation route is opened, the fire threat is mitigated, or evacuees return to their homes. Examples include schools, farms, parks, vineyards, shopping centers or large parking areas where neighborhood citizens may congregate close to their homes.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What is demobilization in emergency response?
ANSWER: Demobilization is the process of standing down response resources in an efficient and timely manner providing considerable cost benefits.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: How is evacuation defined according to the National Incident Management System (NIMS)?
ANSWER: Evacuation is an organized, phased, and supervised withdrawal, dispersal, or removal of civilians from dangerous or potentially dangerous areas, and their reception and care in safe areas.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: What is an Evacuation Order?
ANSWER: An Evacuation Order is a lawful order issued by a jurisdictional authority, to leave immediately due to an immediate threat to life. The area is lawfully closed to public access.

QUESTION NUMBER: 6
QUESTION: What does an Evacuation Warning mean?
ANSWER: An Evacuation Warning is alerting and warning of persons in a defined area of the potential need to evacuate due to threat to life and/or property in response to an emergency. Those who require additional time to evacuate, and those with pets and livestock should leave now.

QUESTION NUMBER: 7
QUESTION: What are Protective Actions in emergency management?
ANSWER: Protective Actions are actions taken by emergency management officials which are designed to protect community members in the event of a wildland urban interface fire. These actions may include directing the public to evacuate, shelter in place, or seek community refuge areas.

QUESTION NUMBER: 8
QUESTION: What is the difference between Re-entry and Repopulation?
ANSWER: Re-entry is the controlled period in which to allow specific residents to return to or survey a previously evacuated area or property, while Repopulation is the return to areas deemed safe by officials where structures are intact, allowing residents and business owners to move back in for long term habitation.

QUESTION NUMBER: 9
QUESTION: What does Shelter-in-Place mean?
ANSWER: Shelter-in-Place is a directive to go indoors, when conditions require that you seek protection in your home, place of employment or other location if a wildfire threatens the area. Community members should be advised by local officials when shelter in place is an option.

QUESTION NUMBER: 10
QUESTION: What are considered Special Facilities in emergency planning?
ANSWER: Special Facilities are facilities which house or serve populations that cannot care for themselves during emergency situations and/or require unique support services. These include schools and day care centers, retirement and nursing homes, and private businesses with large numbers of out-of-town visitors unfamiliar with evacuation procedures.

QUESTION NUMBER: 11
QUESTION: How are Special Needs Populations defined in emergency planning?
ANSWER: Special Needs Populations are individuals in the community with physical, mental, or medical care needs, who may require assistance before, during, and/or after a disaster or emergency after exhausting their usual resources and support network. This may also include economically or culturally isolated populations within the community.

QUESTION NUMBER: 12
QUESTION: What is a Variable Messaging Sign (VMS) and what is it used for?
ANSWER: A Variable Messaging Sign is an electronic traffic sign used on roadways to give travelers information about special events, traffic congestion, roadwork zones, or speed limits on a specific highway segment. They may also ask vehicles to take alternative routes, limit travel speed, warn of duration and location of incidents or inform of traffic conditions.

QUESTION NUMBER: 13
QUESTION: How is Visitor Population defined in emergency planning?
ANSWER: Visitor Population refers to individuals visiting or staying in a place outside their usual place of residence, including workers, employees and business and leisure travelers present in the jurisdiction, whether for single day or overnight stays.

QUESTION NUMBER: 14
QUESTION: What is the role of a Warning Officer?
ANSWER: A Warning Officer is the official responsible for activating various warning notification systems and disseminating public warning information.

QUESTION NUMBER: 15
QUESTION: What is Genasys (formerly Zonehaven) and how is it used in San Mateo County?
ANSWER: Genasys is a commercial software application designed to help first responders manage evacuations during an emergency. It has been purchased by County of San Mateo and is used throughout Portola Valley by public safety agencies to bring fire, law, emergency services and the community together before, during, and after an emergency.

QUESTION NUMBER: 16
QUESTION: What is meant by "Evacuation Order(s) Lifted"?
ANSWER: Evacuation Order(s) Lifted is the formal announcement of lifting evacuations in an area currently under evacuation.

QUESTION NUMBER: 17
QUESTION: What is a Public Warning Notice?
ANSWER: A Public Warning Notice is notification of the public about the need to monitor the media about emergency conditions, special instructions, and/or the need to take protection actions due to a wildfire in the wildland urban interface.

QUESTION: What happens to traffic flow during a contraflow situation in an evacuation?
ANSWER: Contraflow is when vehicles travel in the opposite direction of a lane's normal traffic flow. This occurs during an evacuation, when all traffic lanes move toward safety and away from the hazard. This may restrict all Ingress traffic.

ANSWER: An Ingress Traffic Road is a designated route or roadway specifically for inbound traffic.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4

ANSWER: A Resident only Closure is a soft closure with the additional allowance of residents and local government agencies assisting with response and recovery.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: During a soft closure, what types of vehicles or personnel are allowed to pass through?
ANSWER: A Soft Closure means the road is closed to all traffic except Fire, Law Enforcement and critical incident resources (such as Utility, Caltrans, City/County Roads etc. or those needed to repair or restore infrastructure).

## APPENDIX H – SEISMIC HAZARDS
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What is the primary concern regarding earthquakes in Portola Valley?
ANSWER: The question facing Portola Valley is when—not if—a damaging earthquake will strike, and it will create many road blockages.

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: What are the immediate consequences of a powerful earthquake (magnitude 6 or greater)?
ANSWER: A powerful earthquake will almost certainly block roads, start fires, and may heavily damage water, communications, and energy systems, which will hamper shelter-in-place directives.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What specific hazards can earthquakes create that affect evacuation routes?
ANSWER: Earthquakes can cause direct surface ruptures, strong shaking causing trees and/or power poles to fall across roadways, damage to bridges and culverts, liquefaction of saturated ground, off-fault surface displacements, landslides, and/or mudslides.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: How are faults represented on the evacuation map?
ANSWER: The map uses red lines for faults: heavy solid lines indicate well-documented breaks, while long and short dashes mark progressively less well-proven faults.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: Why might there be undiscovered faults in Portola Valley?
ANSWER: Portola Valley is broadly covered by vegetation and surficial layers of unconsolidated alluvium and soil, which obscure evidence of old surface ruptures, meaning many hidden faults may exist but are yet to be discovered and mapped.

QUESTION NUMBER: 6
QUESTION: What historical example is given of earthquake damage to evacuation routes?
ANSWER: After the magnitude 7.9 (M7.9) 1906 earthquake, ground displacement rendered upper Alpine Road impassable to vehicle traffic, and similar road damage is expected from the next large earthquake on the San Andreas fault system.

QUESTION NUMBER: 7
QUESTION: What is the critical response needed after an earthquake regarding evacuation routes?
ANSWER: The major roads must be immediately cleared of obstructions after the earthquake for people to evacuate, and immediate repair of major roadways will be essential to survival.

QUESTION NUMBER: 8
QUESTION: What specific challenge is mentioned regarding a WFE (wildfire following earthquake) event?
ANSWER: A large fire developing after an earthquake will severely hinder evacuation of the Town, with all evacuation routes potentially impacted by offset roadbeds, broken pavement, and other blockages, impeding both egress and ingress.

QUESTION NUMBER: 9
QUESTION: Will all potential blockages shown on the map occur during one earthquake event?
ANSWER: It is difficult to predict exactly where blockages will occur after a particular earthquake and unlikely that all potential blockages shown on the map will occur during one event.
