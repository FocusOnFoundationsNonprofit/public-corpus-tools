## I. INTRODUCTION
### A. Overview
_mostly the same_
Evacuation, the protective action of moving people and animals out of harm's way, is a critical aspect of emergency response. It is a process that seeks to preserve life, reduce harm, and minimize property damage during potential or actual threats to a community. In planning for evacuation, the characteristics of the hazard and its magnitude, intensity, speed of onset, and anticipated duration are all significant factors.  These will determine the number of people to be evacuated, the distance people must be moved to ensure their safety, the need for reception facilities, and the extent of traffic control and security required. 

Thorough preparation ahead of emergency situations, coupled with effective communication throughout the evacuation process, is of utmost importance in ensuring the safety of our community.  In Portola Valley, the risk of wildfires and earthquakes present the most likely catalysts for emergency evacuation. Given the geographical layout of Portola Valley with only two main ingress and egress routes - Portola and Alpine Roads - the challenges during an evacuation are amplified due to this limited access. Key establishments that require special attention, such as public schools, numerous preschools, Woodside Priory School, and the Sequoias senior living facility, are situated deep within Portola Valley. These institutions, housing some of our most vulnerable population segments, will require specialized evacuation strategies to ensure the safety and well-being of their occupants during emergency situations.

The community must be prepared to conduct both small-scale (e.g., single facility or limited local) and large-scale (e.g., extensive local and regional) evacuations at all times of the day both from known hazard areas and from unexpected incident locations.

### B. Purpose
_major revision - changed from general evacuation (V1) to wildfire-specific (V2)_
The purpose of this plan is to provide for the orderly and coordinated evacuation of all or any part of the population of Portola Valley if it is determined that such action is the most effective means available for protecting the population from the effects of an emergency situation. 

This plan is a snapshot of the Town's current planning and needs to be updated and released frequently as new information is available. During the discussions of the draft of the initial Evacuation Plan, it was identified that an additional chapter and/or appendix needs to be added as soon as possible to provide information for evacuation after an earthquake and possible subsequent events such as wildfire.

We should go back to the good advice from __Dwight D. Eisenhower__, "In preparing for battle I have always found that plans are useless, but planning is indispensable". This plan needs to serve as the educational vehicle for the Town and its residents to prepare and practice for an unforeseen emergency hazard which necessitates an evacuation to take place. Our planning needs to keep up with our changing circumstances and understanding.

### E. Authority
_major revision - changed from ICS-focused (V1) to jurisdictional authority-focused (V2)_
The Incident Command System (ICS) is a standardized, on-scene management approach used for handling any type of incident, regardless of its scale, from inception to resolution. When established the ICS will incorporate all of our emergency response agencies: Woodside Fire, Sheriff, California Highway Patrol, CAL FIRE and the SMC Department of Emergency Management, as well as other involved agencies, departments and public schools. ICS has been the standard for more than 50 years in practice and is used throughout the United States. All of our response agencies have had extensive training and experience in the ICS.

The Incident Commander(s) with jurisdiction from Law Enforcement and/or Fire has the authority to make evacuation and repopulation decisions.  The Incident Commander(s) will work in conjunction with local town officials (Mayor, Town Manager, Town Staff, EOC, CERT, and Town Committees). "Incident Commanders" may be identified by any other agencies or entities with responsibility over a population and specific location, i.e., school, park, business, convalescent home, etc.  These "ICs" or their designees, during an emergency with evacuations, should report to the incoming or on scene Law or Fire Incident Commander and follow their direction.

You will find more information about ICS in Appendix 1.

## II. ROLES, RESPONSIBILITIES, AND LEVELS
### A. Evacuation Decisions
_major revision - changed general overview and roles (V1) to detailed responsibilities by agency (V2); Zonehaven references updated to Genasys; V2 removed Level 1-4 evacuation system_
   1. The Incident Commander shall assess the need for evacuation, plan evacuations, and coordinate support for the evacuation effort. The Incident Commander has the general responsibility for ordering an evacuation, when deemed the most suitable means of protecting the public from a hazard. 

   2. Areas to be evacuated will be determined by those officials with the authority to direct a mandatory evacuation based on the counsel of those individuals and agencies with the necessary expertise, the use of specialized planning materials or decision aids, the recommendations of state and federal agencies, and, where appropriate, advice from other subject matter experts. Evacuation recommendations to the public will clearly describe the area to be evacuated with reference to Zonehaven zones and where needed reference to known geographic features, such as roads and streams.

   3. The hazard situation which gave rise to the need for evacuation should be continually monitored in case changing circumstances, such as an increase in rainfall or wind shift, changes the potential impact area and, thus, the area that must be evacuated.

   4. Authority and Management of an Evacuation - Roles and Responsibilities
		a. During an incident, many agencies will work together to ensure Portola Valley's safety. 
		b. The authority to manage such events may start at the local level. By ordinance, the Town has significant responsibilities for emergency preparedness and planning, per Portola Valley Municipal Code Chapter 2.24 (https://library.municode.com/ca/portola_valley/codes/code_of_ordinances?nodeId=TIT2ADPE_CH2.24EMORPR) . Per that authority, in 2017, the Town Council approved an Emergency Operations Plan (EOP) (www.portolavalley.net/community/emergency-preparedness-information/emergency-operations-plan) that provides the basis for municipal operations during a variety of emergency events, including wildfire. If an ICS is established, primary responsibility will escalate to the Incident Commander, with the Town EOC in a secondary and support role.
		c. Should an incident start in or near town which involves an Incident Commander, this Incident Commander will assume responsibility for the incident and will notify Town staff, which will activate the Emergency Operations Center (EOC), at Town Hall and/or remotely, to manage its operations with the responsible agencies. The EOC utilizes three systems to manage emergencies across multiple agencies and entities: the Standardized Emergency Management System (SEMS); The National Incident Management System (NIMS), and the Incident Command System (ICS); See Appendix 1.
		d. The EOC staff does not decide on the declaration of pre-evacuation/evacuation orders; rather, the Incident Commander, based on information from its responsible agencies on scene, and in coordination with the Town EOC will make such determinations. 
		e. Evacuations that must be conducted, because of incidents that occur without warning, may have to be planned quickly and carried out with only those resources that can be mobilized rapidly.  Pre-incident planning is critical to identifying probable evacuation areas, routes, resources, and tactical operations.  This information must be kept up to date and available to emergency services personnel. Appendix 8 contains the Towns evacuation routes and locations for traffic control. It is critical for mock trial evacuation drills to be executed periodically by the responsible authorities to train and evaluate plans.
   
   5. The Incident Commander, will plan and carry out evacuations and the return of people to their homes or businesses. A large-scale evacuation, however, may require the formation of a regional UC (Unified Command), to support the Incident Command Posts (ICP's) and Emergency Operations Centers (EOC's). This process is well known and practiced in the ICS procedures.
   
   6. Advance Notice of Possible Evacuation (Level 1 and Level 2)
		a. For slow developing emergency situations, advance warning should be given to affected residents as soon as it is clear evacuation may be required. Warning should address suitable preparedness actions, such as securing property, assembling disaster supplies, fueling vehicles, and identifying evacuation routes (section III describes communication means).   
		b. Warning should be made to special facilities in a threatened evacuation area as early as possible. Such facilities should be requested to review and be prepared to implement their evacuation plans. Facility staff should also report their periodic status and any requirements for assistance to the EOC.
		c. The special needs population should also be given advance notice.  Notifying and preparing this segment of the population for evacuation will likely require additional time and resources. Any special circumstances or requests for assistance should be reported to on-scene authorities or EOC.
   
   7. Official Evacuation Warning (Level 3) or Evacuation Order (Level 4)
		a. The decision for an official Evacuation Warning or Order will be made by the Incident Commander, based on information from the responsible agencies on scene. 
   
   8. Agency responsibilities
		a. Portola Valley Town Government – the EOC will be staffed by Town staff and/or appropriately trained volunteers; the EOC will coordinate local personnel and assets, provide local information to residents, and keep track of all activity to ensure appropriate emergency reimbursement at a future date.
		b. Woodside Fire Protection District (WFPD) – serves as the Town's fire department, and will have responsibility for protecting life, property and the environment through Prevention, Education, Preparedness and Emergency response
		c. San Mateo County Sheriff's Office – the Town's contract police services provider; will inform the community of evacuation orders on-site and manage the evacuation with deputies stationed at critical intersections.
		d. In larger incidents Cal Fire, California Highway Patrol, and neighboring communities may become included in the ICS.		
		e. San Mateo County Department of Emergency Management – supports the above agencies with expertise and human resources, including the incident command team.

Should an evacuation be called; state law authorizes offices to restrict access to any area where a menace to public health or safety exists (such as a wildfire). Refusal to comply with such orders is a misdemeanor (Penal Code 409.5). 

### B. Summary of Evacuation Levels
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What are the different evacuation warning levels in the emergency system?
ANSWER: There are four levels: Level 1 (Slow developing emergency situations/Red Flag Warning), Level 2 (Active Fire Nearby or other disaster), Level 3 (Official Evacuation Warning), and Level 4 (Evacuation Order).

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: What is a Level 1 evacuation warning?
ANSWER: Level 1 is a slow developing emergency situation/Red Flag Warning where there is a high hazard situation. In case of fire hazard, there is no active fire in the immediate vicinity but a Red Flag Warning has been issued. During this level, residents should be prepared to evacuate at short notice, EOC is notified, and schools and special needs facilities are notified.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What preparatory actions should residents take during a Level 1 warning?
ANSWER: During a Level 1 warning, residents should: park a car outside the garage facing the street, move the go bag and other items into the car, regularly monitor emergency channels, fill up gasoline tanks for fuel-powered vehicles, and keep battery levels high for electric vehicles.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: What is a Level 2 evacuation warning?
ANSWER: Level 2 indicates an active fire nearby or other disaster with increased readiness due to a greater than normal threat of a hazard that could necessitate evacuation.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: What preparatory actions are taken during a Level 2 warning?
ANSWER: During Level 2, actions include: reviewing information on potential evacuation areas, facilities at risk, and evacuation routes using Zonehaven platform, alerting schools and special needs facilities, monitoring the situation, informing local officials and response personnel, checking evacuation routes and shelter facilities, considering early self-evacuation, and EOC going to level 1 or 2 operation.

QUESTION NUMBER: 6
QUESTION: What is a Level 3 evacuation warning?
ANSWER: Level 3 is an Official Evacuation Warning indicating high readiness due to an increased risk of a hazard which may very shortly necessitate evacuation.

QUESTION NUMBER: 7
QUESTION: What preparatory actions are taken during a Level 3 warning?
ANSWER: Level 3 actions include: monitoring the situation, alerting schools and response personnel, coordinating with special facilities about evacuation readiness, checking resources and transportation assets, activating all communication means, recommending early self-evacuation or preparation for imminent evacuation, and EOC going to level 2 or 3.

QUESTION NUMBER: 8
QUESTION: What is a Level 4 evacuation warning?
ANSWER: Level 4 is an Evacuation Order requiring immediate evacuation of selected Zonehaven areas.

QUESTION NUMBER: 9
QUESTION: What actions are required during a Level 4 evacuation order?
ANSWER: Level 4 actions include: EOC activation to level 3, activation of all communication means, San Mateo County Sheriff and authorities managing the evacuation with deputies at critical intersections, checking evacuation routes, alerting schools and special needs facilities, updating plans for government equipment movement, selecting shelter facilities, and fire agencies preparing to defend critical shelter in place facilities. The EOC itself evacuates if it is in a zone covered by the Evacuation Order.

QUESTION NUMBER: 10
QUESTION: How does the EOC's role escalate across the different evacuation levels?
ANSWER: The EOC's involvement escalates across levels: At Level 1, EOC is notified or alerted; at Level 2, EOC goes to level 1 or 2 operation; at Level 3, EOC goes to level 2 or 3; at Level 4, EOC goes to level 3 and may evacuate if in an evacuation zone.

QUESTION NUMBER: 11
QUESTION: How are schools and special needs facilities handled across all evacuation levels?
ANSWER: Schools and special needs facilities are: notified at Level 1, alerted at Level 2, alerted and coordinated with regarding evacuation readiness at Level 3, and alerted at Level 4.

QUESTION NUMBER: 12
QUESTION: How is self-evacuation addressed throughout the warning levels?
ANSWER: Self-evacuation is recommended at multiple levels: At Level 1, early self-evacuation is highly recommended where possible; at Level 2, early self-evacuation is highly recommended; at Level 3, early self-evacuation is highly recommended, or prepare for imminent evacuation.

This software will especially be used in mock evacuation drills done by the town's responsible agencies.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4

QUESTION: Who controls evacuation movement and how is traffic managed during evacuations?
ANSWER: Actual evacuation movement is controlled by local law enforcement agencies under the direction of the IC. ANSWER: For large-scale evacuations when time permits, traffic control devices, such as signs and barricades, will be provided by the Public Works/Road Department upon request.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: How are disabled vehicles handled during evacuation?
ANSWER: Law enforcement will request wrecker services when needed to clear disabled vehicles from evacuation routes.

#### 4. Emergency Gates - see also Appendix 6
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What is the purpose of emergency gates in Portola Valley?
ANSWER: The town has gates located on former roads that were closed off to prevent daily traffic problems. These gates can be opened to vehicle traffic during an emergency.  

### B. Emergency Public Information, Dissemination and Communication Tools
#### 1. Communication Tools
_mostly the same - same tools mentioned in V2 under Notifications_
The Town and its partners will utilize the following tools to share evacuation messaging:
   a. __SMCAlert__ – the county's emergency alert system can be set up to allow for emails, reverse 911 calls to landlines and text messages. 
   b. __AM 1680__ – the Town's radio station, which can be updated remotely. This communication means covers almost the entire Town and will be operational during power and phone line failures and in vehicles that have AM radio on route during evacuation.
   c. __PVForum__ and __Next Door__
   d. __Town email lists__ and news updates
   e. A __high-low siren__ from Sheriff's vehicles is the approved notice for evacuation.  Residents should make themselves aware of the sound.

It is the responsibility of each resident to ensure they are informed by signing up for or having access to the services that will provide emergency information in an emergency. See section J, Individual Activities for How to Prepare for an Evacuation, for more information.

The Incident Commander will evaluate all situations before releasing a warning or evacuation order. Residents should familiarize themselves with the available communications methods and pay close attention to at least two communications channels when an ICS is activated.

### 7. Pre-Evacuation Preparation Steps 
#### Inside the House
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What is the most important thing to remember about evacuation preparation?
ANSWER: Early evacuation is the most important thing.

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: What interior preparations should be made for evacuation?
ANSWER: Inside the house preparations include: having Emergency Supply Kit and Go Bag ready, knowing community's emergency response plan and evacuation routes, shutting all windows and doors, removing flammable window shades, curtains, and closing metal shutters, removing lightweight curtains, moving flammable furniture to the center of the room away from windows and doors, shutting off gas at the meter, leaving lights on for firefighter visibility, and shutting off air conditioning.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What considerations should be made regarding home security?
ANSWER: If choosing to leave windows and doors unlocked for firefighter access, be advised that looting has occurred in other evacuation incidents.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: What should be done regarding gas utilities?
ANSWER: Shut off gas at the meter and do not turn your gas back on without PG&E present.

#### Outside the house
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What preparations should be made to the exterior of the house?
ANSWER: Outside preparations include: gathering flammable items from house exterior and bringing them inside or placing in pool, turning off propane tanks, moving propane BBQ appliances away from structures, connecting garden hoses to water valves, filling water buckets around the house, leaving exterior lights on, putting Emergency Supply Kit/Go bag in vehicle, backing car into driveway loaded with closed doors and windows, placing a ladder at house corner, sealing attic and ground vents, patrolling property, and checking on neighbors.

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: What precautions should be taken regarding water usage?
ANSWER: Don't leave sprinklers on or water running, as they can affect critical water pressure. However, connect garden hoses to outside water valves or spigots for use by firefighters and fill water buckets to place around the house.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What should be done with vehicles during evacuation preparation?
ANSWER: Back your car into the driveway with the vehicle loaded and all doors and windows closed. Carry your car keys with you.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: What lighting preparations should be made?
ANSWER: Leave your interior lights on so firefighters can see your house under smoky conditions, and leave exterior lights on so your home is visible to firefighters in the smoke or darkness of night.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: When should residents decide to evacuate?
ANSWER: Don't wait for an evacuation order if you feel threatened.

#### Animals
QUESTION NUMBER: 1

ANSWER: Locate pets and keep them nearby. Prepare farm animals for transport and think about moving them to a safe location early.

## APPENDIX 1 - INCIDENT COMMAND SYSTEM (ICS)
### What is ICS?
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What is the Incident Command System (ICS) and where can it be applied?
ANSWER: The Incident Command System is a standardized on-scene, all-hazard management approach used throughout the incident lifecycle. It can be used to manage both emergency and non-emergency situations by government and private-sector organizations, including training conferences, community events, and visits from foreign dignitaries.

### Purpose of ICS
QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: What are the main objectives that the Incident Command System addresses?
ANSWER: The ICS addresses the safety of responders and others, achievement of tactical objectives, efficient use of resources, and lack of accountability including unclear chains of command and supervision.

### History of ICS
QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What historical problems led to the development of ICS?
ANSWER: The ICS was developed due to incident response failures caused by lack of accountability, poor communication and management, lack of a planning process, overloaded Incident Commanders, and no method to integrate interagency requirements.

### Benefits of ICS
QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: What are the key benefits of implementing ICS?
ANSWER: ICS meets needs of incidents of any size, allows personnel from various agencies to merge into a common management structure, provides a preset structure for logistical and administrative functions in emergencies, and is cost effective by avoiding duplication of efforts and overseeing efficient resource use.

### Summary:
QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: What is the Incident Command System (ICS)?
ANSWER: ICS is a standardized management tool for meeting the demands of small or large emergency or nonemergency situations. It represents best practices and has become the standard for emergency management across the country. It may be used for planned events, natural disasters, and acts of terrorism, and is a key feature of NIMS.

### Common Terminology
QUESTION NUMBER: 6
QUESTION: Why is common terminology important in ICS and what does it define?
ANSWER: Common terminology is critical for efficient, clear communication and is used to define organizational functions, incident facilities, resource descriptions, and position titles.

### Use of Plain English
QUESTION NUMBER: 7
QUESTION: What are the requirements for communication in ICS?
ANSWER: ICS requires the use of plain English and prohibits the use of agency-specific codes, radio codes, or jargon.

### Modular Organization
QUESTION NUMBER: 8
QUESTION: How does the modular organization of ICS function?
ANSWER: It develops in a top-down, modular fashion, is based on incident size, complexity and hazard type, with incident objectives determining organizational size. Only necessary functions/positions are filled, and each element must have a person in charge.

### Overall Priorities
QUESTION NUMBER: 9
QUESTION: What are the main priorities in establishing incident objectives in ICS?
ANSWER: The priorities are: 1. Life Saving, 2. Incident Stabilization, 3. Property Preservation.

### Unity of Command
QUESTION NUMBER: 10
QUESTION: What are the key principles of unity of command?
ANSWER: Under unity of command, personnel report to only one supervisor and receive work assignments only from their supervisors.

### Unified Command
QUESTION NUMBER: 11
QUESTION: What is Unified Command and how does it function?
ANSWER: Unified Command creates a single ICS structure for effective multijurisdictional or multiagency approach. Multiple Incident Commanders representing different jurisdictions work together to establish objectives, make joint decisions, and speak as one voice. It enables agencies with different legal, geographic and functional responsibilities to coordinate while maintaining unity of command.

QUESTION NUMBER: 12
QUESTION: What is the key difference between Unity of Command and Unified Command?
ANSWER: Unity of Command means personnel report to only one supervisor and receive work assignments only from their supervisors, while Unified Command is a structure where multiple Incident Commanders from different jurisdictions work together to establish objectives and make joint decisions while maintaining the principle that each employee only reports to one supervisor.

## APPENDIX 3 - TEMPORARY EVACUATION POINTS
### Where to shelter?
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: When should you evacuate in an emergency situation?
ANSWER: In an emergency situation when a dangerous condition arises at your location and it is safe to move to an area which is not hazardous then do so and always evacuate when an evacuation order is issued and transit is safe.

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: When should you move to a shelter-in-place location?
ANSWER: You should move to a shelter-in-place location in situations when your transit to evacuate is not as safe as sheltering in place.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: Under what circumstances should you seek a Temporary Evacuation Point?
ANSWER: You may need to seek a Temporary Evacuation Point when there may not be a permanent shelter-in-place location available, or your transit to evacuate has become blocked.

### Temporary Evacuation Points (TEP)
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What is a Temporary Evacuation Point in a general emergency context?
ANSWER: A Temporary Evacuation Point is a designated location within a building or facility designed to provide a safe and secure space for people during an emergency or crisis situation. It serves as a temporary shelter where individuals can seek protection from immediate hazards such as fires, chemical spills, or natural disasters, while awaiting further instructions or rescue.

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: How is a Temporary Evacuation Point defined specifically in wildfire situations?
ANSWER: In wildfire situations, a Temporary Evacuation Point refers to a designated outdoor location that offers a temporary safe haven for individuals fleeing from approaching flames. These areas are selected based on their proximity to low-risk zones, accessibility, and natural barriers that can provide protection against the wildfire's spread.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What types of locations are typically used as Temporary Evacuation Points during wildfires?
ANSWER: Temporary Evacuation Points in wildfire situations often include open spaces like parks, fields, or cleared areas with minimal vegetation. They are typically located away from the fire's path.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: What support is provided at Temporary Evacuation Points during wildfires?
ANSWER: Emergency personnel and resources are deployed to these areas to provide necessary assistance, such as medical aid, supplies, and coordination of evacuation efforts.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: What are the key guidelines for vehicle evacuation during wildfires?
ANSWER: Stay inside your vehicle even during extreme wildfire conditions, keep windows rolled up, outside vents closed, turn lights on, and drive slowly and carefully. Take the fastest paved route you know best, proceed downhill away from the fire, and know at least two routes.

QUESTION NUMBER: 6
QUESTION: What should you do if trapped during a wildfire evacuation?
ANSWER: Take shelter in a building, car, or an open area; park in an outside turn if trapped on a hillside; stay far from vegetation; look for wide roads, parking lots, playing fields. You are better protected inside a building or vehicle unless a TEP is at hand.

QUESTION NUMBER: 7
QUESTION: What should you avoid doing during a wildfire evacuation?
ANSWER: Don't abandon your car on the road if passage is impossible, don't evacuate by fire road, uphill, or into open-space areas with unburned vegetation, and evacuate on foot only as a last resort.

QUESTION NUMBER: 8
QUESTION: What should you do if you must leave your car during evacuation?
ANSWER: If you must leave your car, park it off the road and consider other options for shelter.

QUESTION NUMBER: 9
QUESTION: Where can detailed evacuation guidelines be found?
ANSWER: Detailed evacuation guidelines can be found at Fire Safe Marin (www.firesafemarin.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/FIRESafe_MARIN_Evacuation_Checklist.pdf).

QUESTION NUMBER: 10
QUESTION: What is the most important mental state to maintain during evacuation?
ANSWER: Remain calm - panic is deadly.

ANSWER: The primary notification will be by SMC Alert. Additional methods include local AM/FM radio stations, social media, loud speakers, reverse 911, and even door-to-door notices.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: What are the different zone status levels in Zonehaven?
ANSWER: The zone status levels are: Evacuation Order (leave now), Evacuation Warning (potential threat), Advisory (be on alert for a threat), Clear to Repopulate (safe to return), Shelter in Place (go indoors and stay), and Normal (no knowledge of threats).

QUESTION NUMBER: 6
QUESTION: What features does Zonehaven's AWARE interface provide for community members?
ANSWER: AWARE provides the ability to know the status of their zone, view the latest updates about active incidents in the area, and find resources to help prepare for an emergency.

QUESTION NUMBER: 7
QUESTION: What capabilities does Zonehaven's EVAC platform provide for first responders?
ANSWER: EVAC allows first responders to prioritize vulnerable areas while understanding dependencies, view or run fire and flood models incorporating weather conditions and geographic data, enable collaboration between agencies for evacuation planning and execution, and send zone alerts and notifications to communities with one click.

QUESTION NUMBER: 8
QUESTION: What should residents do with their zone information once they know it?
ANSWER: Residents should memorize their zone name, write it down and put it in a visible location, such as taped to the inside of their front door. They should also know their zone for where they work or learn.

QUESTION NUMBER: 9
QUESTION: Where can residents find more information about emergency evacuation plans?
ANSWER: Residents can find more information at www.portolavalley.net/evacuations.

QUESTION NUMBER: 10
QUESTION: Where can residents sign up for SMC Alert notifications?
ANSWER: Residents can sign up for SMC Alert notifications at smcalert.info.

### San Mateo County Large Animal Evacuation Group (SMC LAEG)
QUESTION NUMBER: 1
QUESTION: What is SMC LAEG and what is its mission?
ANSWER: San Mateo County Large Animal Evacuation Group (SMC LAEG) is an all-volunteer 501c3 organization that provides evacuation services and shelter for large/farm animals during disasters such as wildfire, flood, or other emergencies. Their mission includes evacuating animals, caring for them in holding areas, and facilitating their return to owners/agents.

QUESTION NUMBER: 2
QUESTION: Who activates SMC LAEG and how is it staffed?
ANSWER: SMC LAEG is activated by the San Mateo County Department of Emergency Management or other first responder unit. Its efforts are staffed by SMC LAEG's core team members and volunteers.

QUESTION NUMBER: 3
QUESTION: What are the four stages of SMC LAEG activation?
ANSWER: Stage One (Initial Contact): Command staff assesses incident scope and resources needed; Stage Two (Alert): Response teams prepare for deployment; Stage Three (Activation): Command Staff notifies response teams to proceed to assignments per Department of Emergency Management Operations Animal Care Branch Director; Stage Four (Reunification & Deactivation): Department Operations Center determines when to deactivate with limited resources held for reunification.

QUESTION NUMBER: 4
QUESTION: What training opportunities does SMC LAEG provide to its members?
ANSWER: SMC LAEG provides: Trailer Maneuvering and Loading, Trailer Safety, Maintenance and Specifications, Large Animal Handling (Basic and Advanced), HAM Radio Operation and Set Up, Activation Drills, Interagency Training with SMC First Responders and Community Resource Teams, Mutual Aid Opportunities, Monthly General Meetings, and Standard Operating Procedures training.

QUESTION NUMBER: 5
QUESTION: What additional services does SMC LAEG offer besides emergency response?
ANSWER: SMC LAEG provides preparedness workshops and site inspections upon request.

QUESTION NUMBER: 6
QUESTION: What preparation steps are recommended for animal owners?
ANSWER: Owners should: be aware, plan escape routes and evacuate early if possible, train animals regularly, inspect surroundings & equipment with regular maintenance, keep information updated, maintain a "Go Kit," keep family/friends/facility management updated, and practice their plan repeatedly.

QUESTION NUMBER: 7
QUESTION: How can SMC LAEG be contacted?
ANSWER: SMC LAEG can be contacted via email at smclaeg@gmail.com, through their website www.smclaeg.org, or by phone at 650-684-0616.
