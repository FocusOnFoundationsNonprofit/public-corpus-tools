CLARIFIED QUESTION: What guidance does the FDA offer for establishing a consolidated primer set for LAMP assays to reduce validation duplication?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: The FDA supports using a consolidated primer set for LAMP assays as a successful pathway. Data related to test performance, such as inclusivity, in silico analysis, and wet cross-reactivity, can be leveraged for authorization. Developers can provide a letter of reference, allowing others to use the test data without repeating all validations. The FDA outlines two pathways: individual authorization with the right of reference or a saliva-direct-style open method where designated labs use the method under an EUA.

QA Block 3:
CLARIFIED QUESTION: Does the FDA require repeated validation for interfering substances if test components like primers and master mix are shared among developers under separate EUAs?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: The FDA focuses on changes introduced by each developer. If a new component is added that was not addressed in the original test, additional validation may be required. Developers can provide a right of reference to share data and avoid duplication, especially if the components like primers or master mix remain the same.

QA Block 8:
CLARIFIED QUESTION: What is the FDA's stance on over-the-counter (OTC) or direct-to-consumer COVID-19 tests that do not require a prescription?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: The FDA has provided recommendations for the validation of OTC or direct-to-consumer COVID-19 tests. While no such test has been authorized yet, the FDA is open to authorizing one. These tests require higher performance standards since a clinician is not involved in interpreting the results, ensuring the consumer can properly act upon the findings.

QA Block 9:
CLARIFIED QUESTION: What software validation requirements are expected for COVID-19 assays under an EUA, especially for AI-driven assays?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: FDA typically does not require the extensive software documentation seen with full authorizations for EUAs. Developers are encouraged to focus on critical components, aiming to eliminate obvious errors leading to inaccurate results. The exact level of validation depends on the software's intended use and context. Early engagement with FDA is recommended to clarify specific expectations and avoid delaying submission.
VERBATIM QUESTION: We were wondering what extent of the software validation would be required?
VERBATIM ANSWER: So for EUAs whenever software is involved they are typically not asking for the extensive software documentation that we would for a full authorization. And we instead want developers to focus on the most critical components prior to authorization. And that will somewhat be in dialogue with the review staff when they know about the details. And so that is - our intent is to at launch have no obvious errors in the software that can lead to false or inaccurate results. So it is going to be a much lower bar for EUAs. And exactly what that - the recommendations are from the FDA is somewhat dependent on the exact software and it's intended use and its point acquired use.
