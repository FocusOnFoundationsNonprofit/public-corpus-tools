QUESTION: What are the guidelines for establishing and authorizing a shared primer set for LAMP assays?
ANSWER: Developers can seek their own individual EUA authorization for a LAMP assay and provide a right of reference for others to copy the test, but this does not extend the original EUA umbrella to those who use the method. Alternatively, developers may follow an open-source model similar to Yale's saliva direct, which includes designating labs to use their authorized method under clear commitments to FDA requirements.

QUESTION: Are developers required to individually validate tests if they use shared primer sets through a letter of reference?
ANSWER: Yes, developers using a shared primer set through a letter of reference are still required to individually validate their tests if they are kit developers. This ensures compliance with EUA authorization rules, as the authorization does not extend to labs or developers merely copying the method.

QUESTION: What steps and FDA requirements exist for laboratories to gain designation under an open-source EUA such as SalivaDirect?
ANSWER: A sponsor granted an EUA for an open-source method can designate participating laboratories under their EUA. The sponsor must ensure those labs comply with predefined FDA commitments. These include following specific instructions for use, sourcing components as specified in the EUA (e.g., from FDA-authorized suppliers), and adhering to any data-sharing requirements established by the EUA holder.

QUESTION: What are the requirements for a test developer working with another developer to consolidate primer sets and master mix while seeking separate EUAs?
ANSWER: When two test developers consolidate on the same primer set and master mix, they may still need to repeat some aspects of validation, such as testing for interfering substances, unless this data is already covered under a granted right of reference and satisfies FDA requirements. Each developer can independently seek their own EUA and control designations, even if a general right of reference is granted by one to the other.

QUESTION: What are the implications if two test developers consolidate on the same primer set and LAMP master mix while pursuing independent EUAs for different purification methods?
ANSWER: If two developers consolidate on the same primer set and LAMP master mix but pursue independent EUAs for different purification methods, certain test alterations may require additional validation. ANSWER: For artificial intelligence in COVID-19 assays, the FDA requires comprehensive software validation, including testing analysis, requirements and design specifications, and a full validation suite.
