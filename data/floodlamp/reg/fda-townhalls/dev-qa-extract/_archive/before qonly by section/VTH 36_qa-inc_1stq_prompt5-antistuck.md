QA Block 3:
### QUESTION: What guidance does the FDA provide regarding establishing a primer set for LAMP assays to simplify validation for multiple developers?
ANSWER: The FDA recommends that developers coordinating on a primer set for LAMP assays can reference a general letter of reference similar to those used by the CDC for PCR primary sets. Such coordination can reduce individual validation repetition. Developers must ensure inclusivity analysis, in silico cross-reactivity, and wet cross-reactivity testing meet required standards. Specific guidance can vary depending on the assay, and developers are encouraged to consult the FDA's resources for detailed validation requirements.

QA Block 4:
### _QUESTION: What steps should developers take to consolidate and use a primer set for LAMP assays to streamline test validation and authorization?_
ANSWER: Developers can pursue two pathways to consolidate and use a primer set for LAMP assays. First, developers can seek individual authorization for their kit and provide others the right of reference to copy and use the data from their submission, provided the same suppliers and catalog numbers are used. Second, a method can be authorized, following models like Yale's SalivaDirect, which provides flexibility for designated labs under their EUA, with clear commitments to the FDA regarding designation requirements.

QA Block 6:
### QUESTION: What processes can developers follow to consolidate a primer set for LAMP assays and manage inclusivity and cross-reactivity analyses?
ANSWER: Developers can consolidate a primer set for LAMP assays by seeking their own test authorization, allowing other developers to reference their application while maintaining separate authorizations, or by pursuing an open-source method similar to Yale's SalivaDirect. Inclusivity and in silico cross-reactivity analyses must align with FDA requirements. Developers can use authorized primers, such as those listed under CDC authorization, to simplify validation. Any changes to methods or suppliers would require separate evaluation. Open-source methods can designate specific labs under their authorization, but such flexibility requires adherence to FDA commitments.

### QUESTION: Can point-of-care facilities use venous draw whole blood for antibody test kits authorized for fingerstick use, and are there limitations for plasma and serum?
ANSWER: Point-of-care facilities can use venous draw whole blood for antibody test kits authorized for fingerstick use. However, using plasma and serum typically involves additional steps not suited for point-of-care settings, as they generally lack the training and equipment needed for such processes.

QA Block 12:
### QUESTION: What are FDA's requirements for software validation in relation to artificial intelligence used in COVID-19 diagnostics and the handling of end-user instrument calibration?
ANSWER: FDA requires software used in COVID-19 diagnostics, including AI-based methods, to undergo thorough validation. This includes providing documentation such as test analysis, requirement specifications, design specifications, and full validation suites. If the AI algorithm is calibrated by the end user to their instrument, the calibration set should be incorporated into a change plan, such as a software as a medical device (SaMD) pre-specification or an algorithm change protocol. While the molecular template mentions software should be validated as free of defects, FDA acknowledges the reality that no software is entirely defect-free and expects reasonable efforts to minimize defects in validation.

QA Block 13:
### _QUESTION: What is FDA’s general guidance on software validation requirements for EUAs involving artificial intelligence in COVID-19 assays?_
ANSWER: For EUAs involving software, including artificial intelligence, FDA generally does not require the extensive software documentation needed for full authorization. Developers should prioritize addressing critical components to ensure there are no obvious errors that could result in false or inaccurate results. The exact requirements depend on the software's intended use and point of use. Early and ongoing engagement with FDA is advised to clarify specific validation needs.
