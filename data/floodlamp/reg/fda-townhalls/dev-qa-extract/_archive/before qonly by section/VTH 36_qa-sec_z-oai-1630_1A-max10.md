CLARIFIED QUESTION: How can a primer set for LAMP-based tests be consolidated and referenced under EUA for multiple developers?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: Developers can pursue two pathways for EUA designation: they can obtain individual authorization for their kit and provide a right of reference for others, or they can develop an open-source model similar to Yale's SalivaDirect, where designated labs are covered under the EUA. Each approach has flexibility, but changes to components or procedures may require additional validation. The FDA evaluates such changes to ensure adherence to standards.

So looking at the saliva direct model they have in their original submission and any amendments -- and I have to check what amendments they have to date -- they have an instruction for use that is the sole basis so to speak of the kit. CLARIFIED QUESTION: Do test developers need to repeat validation for interfering substances if pursuing an EUA with differing purification methods?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: Developers may need to provide additional validation if the test includes alterations, such as different purification methods, even under the same EUA authorization.
