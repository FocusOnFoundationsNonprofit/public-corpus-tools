QA Block 1
CLARIFIED QUESTION: What are the recent FDA authorizations related to COVID-19 test development and their priorities?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: Recent FDA authorizations include more pooling authorizations for identifying asymptomatic cases in populations with low disease prevalence, home collection kits with a SARS-CoV-2 and flu panel, another rapid antigen test, and additional serology tests. The FDA’s current review and development priorities include point-of-care tests, home collection kits, panels, and high throughput systems for large sample volumes.

QA Block 3
CLARIFIED QUESTION: What guidance does the FDA provide on using a consolidated primer set for colorimetric LAMP-based assays, including how developers can coordinate and share validations?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: The FDA recognizes consolidating on a primer set and leveraging validations is a viable pathway. Two main approaches are available: (1) obtaining authorization for a kit and granting a right of reference for other developers to use; while each would still need their own authorization for variations, using identical suppliers and components simplifies the process. (2) Following the saliva direct model, where an instructional method is authorized, giving flexibility for sponsors to designate labs covered under their EUA. The Yale model has been highly successful, with specific commitments to ensure compliance.

CLARIFIED QUESTION: What happens if two test developers using the same primer set and LAMP master mix have independent EUAs and give cross-references?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: The FDA recognizes that test developers can grant rights of references, specifying the extent of these rights. If components used are the same, it reduces duplication. However, changes such as new purification methods may require additional validation. Developers can choose to include limitations or open rights entirely, fostering synergy and reducing redundancy when tests share core components.

CLARIFIED QUESTION: What are the FDA's guidelines for coordinating on a consolidated LAMP primer set for open-source diagnostic tests, covering inclusivity and cross-reactivity validation?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: The FDA acknowledges that using a centralized primer set can be efficient. Inclusivity, in silico and wet cross-reactivity must be carefully validated. For open-source tests, such as the Yale SalivaDirect model, FDA-approved primers and catalog numbers must be used consistently to align with authorization.
