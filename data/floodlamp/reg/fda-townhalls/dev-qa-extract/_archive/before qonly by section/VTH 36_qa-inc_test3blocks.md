VERBATIM QUESTION: Hi there Dr. Stenzel. Thank you for doing these town halls. They are very useful. I have a question about establishing a primer set for LAMP. They - I’ve - I’m with a public benefit corporation called FloodLAMP and we are developing a colorimetric LAMP-based assay that has a ultracheap front end and then goes into the colorimetric LAMP. And we're coordinating with other test developers who also want to open source our test to provide very low cost scalable options for essentially exactly the scenario that you described above in terms of the screening of - repeated screening of asymptomatic population. So to pursue these together it's going to be very efficient to consolidate on primer set. And for - if we establish a primer set and give a general letter of reference that the CDC did for their primary sets for PCR then it’s my understanding we wouldn’t - each of us individually wouldn't have to repeat certain aspects of the validation. And I just want to understand that dynamic a bit more in terms of the inclusivity in silico analysis and the in silico cross-reactivity and the wet cross-reactivity. Can you sort of speak to that and give us some sort of guidance as we coordinate on this?
VERBATIM ANSWER: Timothy Stenzel: Yeah, thanks for that question. And thank you for your work on developing a LAMP-based test and for pursuing cost-efficient scalable testing solutions. The key to simplifying the validation process while ensuring accuracy is indeed collaboration and sharing standardized materials like primer sets. The general concept you’re proposing, where test developers agree on a common primer set and then reference this in their documentation, can help avoid redundant validation efforts. However, each test developer would still need to perform some level of validation to ensure that their specific test performance meets regulatory requirements. This would include inclusivity and cross-reactivity studies. It’s important to perform these in silico and wet lab cross-reactivity tests to confirm the primer set’s broad applicability and lack of cross-reactivity with other pathogens. So, while a lot can be streamlined through collaboration, each developer must still ensure their test's validation aligns with FDA standards.
CLARIFIED QUESTION: Can you provide guidance on how we can use a common primer set to avoid redundant validation for our LAMP-based assays and the required validation steps for inclusivity and cross-reactivity?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: Each test developer can agree on a common primer set and reference this in their documentation to help avoid redundant validation efforts. However, each developer still needs to perform some level of validation, including inclusivity and cross-reactivity studies, both in silico and wet lab, to ensure the test meets FDA standards and lacks cross-reactivity with other pathogens.

VERBATIM QUESTION: And I guess just one quick follow-up question. So if we get authorization for a test with a certain license or activation buffer and a certain purification like we're pursuing an ultracheap class mill purification and but we're working with another test developer who is seeking authorization for a mag B based purification it would be highly amenable to automation. And so if we consolidated on the same primer set and LAMP master mix then would they need to - they wouldn’t - it's my understanding they would need to repeat the upfront parts, but with respect to the interfering substances would they need to repeat that? And then if we gave them a general right of reference and they would end up getting their own EUA that they could control the designations on as well. And then we would have our - and we would have our own independent EUA that we control the designations on even though we granted a right of reference. Is that how it would work?
VERBATIM ANSWER: Tim Stenzel: So that's a very good question as well. Once you change anything in the process, even if it’s just a different purification method or different sample prep method, you will need to repeat some of the validation studies specific to that change. This includes testing for interfering substances. Each change, such as a new purification method, could introduce new variables that must be validated to ensure the continued performance of the test. So yes, even if there is standardization on the same primer set and LAMP master mix, any major component change indeed necessitates revalidation to confirm the test’s accuracy and reliability remain intact. And yes, you would be able to grant them right of reference for them to seek their EUA. They would then manage their designation independently while you manage yours.
CLARIFIED QUESTION: If we change the purification method in our test but use the same primer set and LAMP master mix, do we need to repeat the validation studies for interfering substances? Also, if we grant another developer the right of reference, can we each control our own EUA designations?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: Yes, any change in the process, such as a different purification method, requires repeating validation studies for interfering substances and other variables to ensure test performance. You can grant a right of reference to another developer, allowing them to seek their own EUA and manage their designation independently while you control your own.
