CLARIFIED ANSWER: Factors that can influence the EUA timeline include the triage system prioritizing applications based on test type (e.g., home collection tests, point-of-care tests, and high-throughput testing), completeness of submissions, and their significance to the national response effort. Developers can check the completeness and triage status of their submission by contacting the assigned contact within two weeks of submission. The FDA processes over 2400 applications, with significant progress made, including tests reviewed by NCI, which helps address backlogs.

QA Block 19
CLARIFIED QUESTION: What is the FDA's position on off-label use of antigen tests for asymptomatic screening, including handling false positives and developer guidance?
CLARIFIED ANSWER: The FDA acknowledges mixed data on off-label use of antigen tests for asymptomatic screening. Developers seeking claims for such use must submit supporting data, with options like comparative studies or historical data. Clinicians are allowed to prescribe off-label tests as stated in FAQs, ensuring compliance with CLIA and CMS. False positives occur more frequently in low-prevalence populations due to specificity limits, and positive results should be confirmed with orthogonal testing. Despite challenges, such testing identifies true positives faster, serving as an important public health tool.
VERBATIM QUESTION: Just curious if the FDA has any updated position on the off-label use of antigen testing for asymptomatic screening given how forceful HHS was in requiring the model to resume the use of antigen testing in nursing homes for that purpose? Does the FDA have a position on this -- it's off label use and more importantly are you aware of any studies to measure the performance of the antigen testing in the asymptomatic population?
VERBATIM ANSWER: Yes, the data we’ve received -- and I can’t go into all of it because much of it is confidential -- is mixed.
